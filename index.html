What does a stated privacy budget mean for a patient with many scans?

A model trained with DP-SGD states one privacy budget ε per record. A patient whose twin holds k MRI sessions contributes k records. This calculator prints that patient's receipt: the stated budget, the worst-case bound for all k sessions together, and the budget under user-level training.

Bounds are upper limits from Rényi-DP accounting (validated against Google's dp-accounting). The real leakage for you lies somewhere between the stated budget and your bound. Measuring where is what the per-person audit in this project does.

How the bound grows with the number of sessions

Your bound, record-level training Stated and user-level budget

Why the bound grows so fast. In each step, record-level DP-SGD clips each record's gradient to norm C and adds noise of scale σC. If all k of your sessions land in one batch and their gradients point the same way, your total influence is kC against the same noise. Sessions of one brain share the anatomy, so aligned gradients are a realistic worst case, not a far-fetched one.

How the bound is computed. You are included in a step with probability p = 1 − (1 − q)^k. Your contribution is then bounded by a Gaussian with noise multiplier σ/k. The calculator runs the standard RDP accountant for the subsampled Gaussian on (p, σ/k), over integer orders 2 to 512, and converts to (ε, δ). The attack ceiling is the most a membership test can achieve at a 1% false-positive rate: min(1, e^ε · 0.01 + δ).

What user-level training changes. It samples and clips per person instead of per record, so your k sessions together count once. At the same sampling rate and noise, your budget equals the stated one, whatever k is. The cost is accuracy, which the project measures.
